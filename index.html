LEARN FROM THE PAST

Seerah, Prophets & Islamic History

A structured journey through the Prophet’s life, Qur’anic history and the early Muslim community. Dates in the early Seerah are approximate where historians differ.

Seerah of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ

1. Early life in Makkah

Born in Makkah around 570 CE, Muhammad ﷺ grew up an orphan, was cared for by his grandfather and then his uncle Abu Talib, and became known for trustworthiness. He married Khadijah رضي الله عنها, who supported him when revelation began.

Further reading: Qur’an 93:6–8; Sahih al-Bukhari 3

2. The first revelation

At about forty years of age, the first revelation came in the cave of Hira. The opening verses of Surah al-‘Alaq call humanity to read in the name of its Creator. Khadijah comforted and supported the Prophet ﷺ.

Qur’an 96:1–5; Sahih al-Bukhari 3

3. Calling people to Allah

The early Muslims faced hostility and persecution. Some emigrated to Abyssinia for safety. The message emphasised worship of one God, justice, care for the vulnerable and moral responsibility.

Qur’an 16:90; 107:1–7; historical Seerah accounts

4. Isra’ and Mi‘raj

The Qur’an describes the Night Journey from al-Masjid al-Haram to al-Masjid al-Aqsa. Authentic Hadith narrate the Ascension and the obligation of the five daily prayers.

Qur’an 17:1; Sahih al-Bukhari 349

5. Hijrah to Madinah

In 622 CE, the Prophet ﷺ emigrated to Madinah. The new Muslim community built a mosque, strengthened bonds of mutual support and developed shared responsibilities.

Qur’an 9:40; 59:9

6. Badr and Uhud

The Battle of Badr took place in 624 CE and Uhud in 625 CE. The Qur’an uses these events to teach trust in Allah, discipline, patience and accountability.

Qur’an 3:121–179; 8:5–19

7. The Treaty of Hudaybiyyah

In 628 CE, the Muslims concluded a truce with Quraysh. Although some terms appeared difficult, the treaty opened new opportunities for peaceful contact.

Qur’an 48:1–29; Sahih al-Bukhari 2731–2732

8. The opening of Makkah

In 630 CE, the Prophet ﷺ entered Makkah with the Muslims. The Ka‘bah was cleansed of idols and the city became a centre of monotheistic worship.

Qur’an 110:1–3; Sahih al-Bukhari 4287

9. Farewell pilgrimage and final days

In 632 CE, the Prophet ﷺ performed his Farewell Pilgrimage, teaching the rites of Hajj and emphasising the dignity and responsibilities of believers. He died later that year in Madinah.

Qur’an 5:3; Sahih Muslim 1218

Deeper Seerah study: events and lessons

Open each topic to explore the event, its lesson and a primary reference. Historical dates and narrative details should be checked against recognised Seerah scholarship.

Early public preaching in Makkah

The Prophet ﷺ invited his community to worship Allah alone. The earliest Muslims faced pressure, yet the message emphasised accountability, mercy and justice. Reflect on how patience and sound character support calling others to goodness.

Qur’an 26:214; 15:94; 74:1–7
Opposition, hardship and steadfastness

The early Muslim community endured opposition. Qur’anic guidance repeatedly called for patience, prayer and reliance on Allah rather than abandoning principles under pressure.

Qur’an 94:5–6; 16:127; 29:2–3
The migration to Madinah

The Hijrah marked a new chapter in community life. It reminds us that faith can require sacrifice and that people who offer refuge and support play an essential role in society.

Qur’an 9:40; 59:9
Brotherhood and responsibilities in Madinah

The Qur’an praised those who welcomed and supported migrants. A lasting lesson is to help newcomers, care for vulnerable neighbours and build communities around trust and shared responsibility.

Qur’an 59:9–10; 49:10
The Battle of Badr and lessons of humility

Badr is discussed in the Qur’an as a moment of divine help and human responsibility. Study it without romanticising warfare; reflect on humility, discipline and avoiding arrogance.

Qur’an 3:123–125; 8:5–19
Uhud and learning from setbacks

The Qur’an discusses hardship, mistakes, consultation and forgiveness following Uhud. Even serious setbacks can become occasions for reflection, repentance and renewed responsibility.

Qur’an 3:121–180, especially 3:152–159
Hudaybiyyah and the value of peace

The Treaty of Hudaybiyyah offers lessons in patience, diplomacy and the long-term value of peaceful agreements. Study the historical account together with Surah al-Fath.

Qur’an 48:1–29; Sahih al-Bukhari 2731–2732
The Farewell Sermon and human dignity

Teachings from the Prophet’s final pilgrimage emphasise the sanctity of life and property, accountability and the responsibility to treat others justly. Compare specific quotations with reliable Hadith reports rather than circulating unattributed versions.

Sahih Muslim 1218; Qur’an 5:3

Companions and Islamic civilisation

Abu Bakr رضي الله عنه: the first caliph and a close companion of the Prophet ﷺ (Qur’an 9:40). ‘Umar رضي الله عنه: the second caliph, remembered for administrative development and justice. ‘Uthman رضي الله عنه: the third caliph, associated with the standardisation and distribution of Qur’anic codices (Sahih al-Bukhari 4987). ‘Ali رضي الله عنه: the fourth caliph, renowned for learning and courage. ‘A’ishah رضي الله عنها: an important transmitter of Hadith and teacher of Islamic knowledge.

Later Muslim societies developed major centres of scholarship in Madinah, Baghdad, Cairo, Cordoba and elsewhere. Their histories include achievements, disagreements and challenges that deserve careful study rather than simplified legends.

OUR STORY · OUR SERVICE · OUR UMMAH

Worldwide Islamic Prayer Family Foundation

From Qur’an teaching in 2016 to a worldwide community of worship, education, service and compassion.

Our beginning: faith learned, faith shared

Worldwide Islamic Prayer Family Foundation traces its beginnings to Qur’an classes started in 2016. Over time, those lessons developed into a wider community dedicated to Islamic learning, midnight prayer, Sunday Asalatu gatherings, mutual assistance and practical charity.

The foundation’s founder, Ustaz Ibrahim Ademola Abdulwahab, studied Adult Education and Islamic Studies at the University of Ibadan. He credits his late father, Sheikh Abdulwahab Abdulkarim Keulere, with teaching him to pray from childhood and shaping the religious knowledge and love of worship that inspire his work today.

Members and supporters take part from Nigeria and other countries, including a significant community in the United States. The foundation recognises Alhaja Rashidat Agbaji Adetola as its Amirah in America, alongside the many volunteers and members contributing their time, prayers and resources.

Our service is guided by compassion and human dignity. Help is offered to people in need without excluding them because they are Muslim or Christian.

Every Child in School Initiative

Founded before Ustaz Ibrahim Ademola Abdulwahab entered university, the Every Child in School Initiative grew from identifying children of disadvantaged families who were out of school and helping them access education. The foundation continues to support learners, including students at the University of Ibadan and other educational institutions in Nigeria.

Charity, healthcare and community development

Food and family relief

Providing food and practical assistance to households experiencing hardship, including widows and children from low-income families.

Medical and surgical assistance

Helping families with hospital expenses, operations and surgery. The foundation reports supporting a person injured by gunfire in the leg during the #EndSARS period in Nigeria. Beneficiary details are withheld to protect privacy.

Mosque building and repair

Supporting the construction and rehabilitation of mosques in Nigerian communities, as well as donating copies of the Qur’an, sound systems and other worship equipment.

Clean water and boreholes

Supporting community borehole projects to improve access to water for residents, regardless of religious background.

Widows and vulnerable children

Helping widows, orphans and children of less privileged households with essential needs, education and community support.

Prayer and Islamic education

Qur’an classes, Sunday Asalatu, midnight prayer, religious reminders and opportunities for members worldwide to learn and worship together.
